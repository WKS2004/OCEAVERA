# Sampling design review

Read when defining or auditing constructed comparison samples.

| Decision | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Target semantics | Observed presences, survey design and any genuine absence information |
| Accessible domain | Marine boundary, species ecology, sampling coverage and intended prediction region |
| Comparison scheme | Background or pseudo-absence rationale, exclusion assumptions and bias controls |
| Independence | Repeated observations, shared environmental cells, spatial concentration and fold membership |
| Reproducibility | Resource/dataset versions, sample origin, seed, counts and generating artefact |
| Sensitivity | Plausible domains, sample ratios or bias treatments and consequences for comparison |

Do not prescribe a universal sample ratio, distance buffer or thinning threshold before evidence exists. A blanket prohibition on background/presence-cell overlap can alter the target and spatial bias; define and justify the overlap policy for the chosen method rather than copying a generic rule.

Scores depend on the constructed comparison and observation process. Calibration against those labels alone does not establish true occurrence probability. Record assumptions, geographic limits and uncertainty alongside the target design.
